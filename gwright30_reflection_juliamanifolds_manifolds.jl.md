# juliamanifolds_manifolds.jl

## Reflection

Manifolds.jl didn't have any full months without commits, so there isn't an inactivity gap to investigate. It had 8,262 commits in the data, and the biggest concentration of activity was around 2020. Later years are quieter, which is why I classified the trend as declining. That describes the volume, though, not whether the project is alive.

The recent GitHub history makes that distinction pretty clear. There are still commits adding functionality, including work on the Veronese manifold, along with fixes to statistics-related code and dependency updates. So I wouldn't look at the lower bars after 2020 and conclude development stopped. It seems more accurate to say that the level of activity changed.

This one was relatively straightforward because there wasn't a mystery gap to explain, but the graph still needed some care. The available 2026 data only covers part of the year, so comparing its total against a full year would make the decline look worse than it really is. I also can't determine how much work each commit represents just from the count.
